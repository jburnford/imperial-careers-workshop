Cleaning and Structuring Historical Data: A Working Session

AI and History Conference, Johns Hopkins University. Thursday 15 October 2026, 1:30–2:30 p.m., Room 110, SNF Agora Institute. Streamed on Zoom.

Draft

Working draft for Jim Clifford’s review. Source and data: github.com/jburnford/trimen-workshop. Facts about the sources have been checked where a page says so; everything else is a proposal.

Three questions

A historian turning a text into data asks three questions of it, and each has one decision only the historian can make. A model can do everything around that decision.

The question The model does You decide
Clean Is this what the page says? Flags what reads oddly Read the page and rule
Structure What is a record here? Proposes fields, fills them, keeps the quotes What counts, and whether the record changed the meaning
Ground Who is this? Retrieves candidates with evidence Supported, unresolved, or wrong

That is the whole lesson. The hour illustrates it with one page.

One page

Walter Agar’s letter, The Tropical Agriculturist, 2 July 1883, p. 66 (scan). Two hundred and thirty-four words from a planters’ quarrel about one cinchona tree. It contains:

  • a sentence the OCR got wrong in a way that reverses its meaning (clean);
  • a relationship the journal’s editor states loosely and Agar states carefully (structure);
  • a person with a strong identity, Henry Trimen, Director of the Royal Botanic Gardens at Peradeniya, and a person with none in any authority file, Agar himself (ground).

You do not need to know anything about cinchona. The dispute is context for thirty seconds.

The hour

Minutes
0–5 The page Where Trimen comes from, and the letter
5–20 Structure The model proposes a table and extracts records. Then you: did a careful statement become a flat one?
20–30 Ground The model retrieves candidates. Then you: supported, unresolved, or wrong?
30–45 Clean, together The OCR against the page, and what the correction becomes
45–55 Exercise Three decisions, in the room or on Zoom
55–60 Your own data Taking the three questions to another collection

Every live stage has a saved checkpoint in outputs/.

What you need

A browser. The exercise runs from saved results and needs no account or key. Remote participants have the same pages and the same exercise; the exercise page has a button that copies a one-line summary of your decisions for the Zoom chat.
