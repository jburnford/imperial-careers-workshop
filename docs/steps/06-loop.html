The map finds the errors

Minutes 40–47. Three errors seen in the atlas, what caused them, and what fixing them changed.

A wrong grounding is invisible in a table of 180,000 rows. On a map it is a dot in the wrong hemisphere. The project keeps a running list of defects surfaced while building the atlas, and a review of 3 September 2026 that re-fetched every QID in the graph and loaded the atlas headlessly to look for more. Three from those lists.

A premier of British Columbia in Australia

Harlan Brewster’s 1918 entry (saved): newspaper work in New Brunswick and Boston, removed to British Columbia in 1897, elected for Alberni, “defeated by Sir Richard McBride, in Victoria, 1912”, re-elected at a by-election in 1916, premier of British Columbia 1916. The graph gives him two events as member for Victoria. The bare surface “Victoria” was grounded to the Australian colony, Q56850459, by the surface-keyed cache. The atlas showed a British Columbia premier with a seat in Melbourne.

Seen on the map. Cause: a cache keyed on the surface string, with “Victoria” resolved once for everyone. Fix: a per-person context resolver. If the official’s other postings signal British Columbia, “Victoria” is British Columbia (Q1973), which geolocates to its seat; otherwise the colony. Also found while fixing it: per-person overrides had never applied at all, because the resolver keyed people one way and the graph another. Repairing that fixed a second, unrelated split between the North-Western Provinces of India and of Ceylon. Both corpora re-emitted.

The Union of South Africa at Harare

Five surfaces, “Union of S. Africa”, “S. Africa”, “S.A.” and two more, had been joined to the Wikidata item for the British South Africa Company’s territory, Q5155572, because the join matched “South Africa” by containment. 960 Colonial Office events, 283 arcs in and 289 out, plotted at Harare.

Seen in the September review, on the map, as a node with far too many arcs for Rhodesia. Cause: a containment match in the manifest join. Fix: five cache rows patched to Q193619 Union of South Africa, a containment guard added to the join so it cannot recur, and the note that pre-1910 “S. Africa” is ambiguous and about 100 events still need a date-aware rule. The patched rows carry the reason (cache rows).

Two Baluchistans

The colony layer had Baluchistan under Q843, which is Pakistan, for fifty India Office events, and a second Baluchistan node under a different QID. The atlas drew two nodes with the same label. Fix: one canonical entry in the colony table, the duplicate folded in. The same review found Penang grounded to the modern Malaysian state (1,083 events; label-locked so it looked right) and “Dutch East Indies” attracting “New Guinea” and “Australasia”.

What a fix changes

Each fix is to a rule or a cache row, not to a record, so it applies to everyone the rule touched. After each one the pipeline re-emits both corpora and the atlas is rebuilt. The review then checks two things: the cases the fix was meant to repair, and the previously accepted cases that changed. The second group is where new errors hide.

The same discipline for structure: the every-tenth audit’s error catalogue became a post-processing pass with tests, and the three name probes that found the dedup defects are regression tests. The probe that found the error is the test that keeps it out.

What the map cannot find

False negatives. An official whose “Victoria” was left ungrounded does not appear anywhere wrong; he simply has no arc. A merge that should have happened leaves two short careers instead of one long one, both plausible. For those the project built the silver standards: 200 hand-labelled cases, stratified so that the risky strata (rare surname, overlapping eras) are oversampled, scored against the machine output. On the Colonial Office List never-biographied population the judge’s “different” verdicts in the rare-surname stratum turned out to have a false-negative rate of 76 per cent, which no map would have shown.
